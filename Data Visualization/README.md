# Visualization Report

## Project Overview
This project examines the performance of an e-commerce business across sales, products, customers, marketing channels, promotions, and order outcomes.
The aim was to move beyond simply looking at total sales and understand what is driving revenue, where potential sales are being lost, and which areas of the business deserve more attention.
The analysis covers transactions from 2023 to 2025 and brings the findings together in an interactive dashboard designed to give a quick view of business performance and the areas that may require action.


## Problem Statement
The analysis was built around seven key business questions:

- What is the total net revenue and order count, excluding cancelled and returned orders?
- Which products generate the highest total revenue, and how many units were sold?
- What is the monthly revenue trend across the dataset?
- Who are the top 10 highest-spending customers, and how many orders have they placed?
- What is the purchase drop-off rate between items added to cart and units purchased, and how does it vary by month?
- Which referral channels drive the most revenue and have the highest average order value?
- How do promotional coupons perform in terms of order volume and revenue, and what is the financial impact of cancelled and returned orders?

The dashboard brings the answers to these questions into one view to quickly understand the health of the business.

### Sales Performance
The two KPI cards at the top provide the starting point. The business recorded 703 completed orders and 745,088 in net revenue, after excluding cancelled and returned orders. This gives us the baseline for understanding the business: there is a solid level of sales activity, but the other visuals help explain what is happening behind those numbers.


### Monthly Revenue Performance
The monthly revenue trend shows that performance fluctuates considerably across the three-year period rather than following a steady upward path. The strongest point on the dashboard is June 2024, with revenue of 68,069, while one of the lowest points is April 2023 at 27,752. For the business, this raises an important question: what was happening during the stronger months that could potentially be repeated? Understanding these changes can help management plan promotions, inventory and marketing activity around periods of stronger demand.


### Purchase Drop-Off
The purchase drop-off trend shows the difference between what customers added to their carts and what they eventually purchased. The rate remains relatively high throughout the period, generally sitting around 40%–51%. This is an important business signal. Customers are showing buying interest, but a significant portion of that interest does not result in a purchase. Rather than only focusing on attracting more visitors, the business has an opportunity to improve the journey from "I want this" to "I bought this."


### Product Revenue Ranking
The product ranking shows that Chair and Printer are the leading revenue-generating products, at approximately 195.6K each. They are followed closely by Laptop, Tablet and Monitor. The relatively close revenue levels across the products suggest that the business has a reasonably balanced product portfolio, rather than depending entirely on one product. The top-performing products could be used more strategically through product placement, bundles and cross-selling.


### Revenue by Referral Channel
The referral chart shows that Instagram generates the highest revenue at approximately 275.3K. Email follows at about 261.8K, while Google and Facebook are both around 250.4K. Referral traffic contributes approximately 226.8K. However, revenue is not the only useful measure here. Facebook has the highest average order value, meaning customers coming through that channel tend to spend more per order. This tells management that different channels may be valuable for different reasons: Instagram drives more total revenue, while Facebook attracts higher-value orders.


### Top-Spending Customers
The customer ranking highlights the 10 customers contributing the highest spending. The highest spender is C38840, with 5,723 in total spending. The remaining top customers are relatively close, with most spending between 3.3K and 3.5K. These customers represent an important group for the business because retaining existing high-value customers can be just as important as acquiring new ones.


### Coupon Performance
The coupon analysis shows that FREESHIP generated the highest revenue at approximately 335.0K, followed by SAVE10 at 304.8K and WINTER15 at 302.5K. FREESHIP was the strongest-performing coupon, generating approximately 335K in revenue, ahead of SAVE10 (304.8K) and WINTER15 (302.5K). This shows that free shipping was the most successful promotion among the coupons used. Given the high purchase drop-off seen across the business, it would be worth testing whether free-shipping offers can also help encourage more customers to complete their purchases.


### Cancelled vs Returned Orders
The final section highlights the financial impact of unsuccessful orders. Cancelled orders account for approximately 276.4K, while returned orders account for approximately 243.3K. Together, they represent a substantial amount of order value that did not become retained sales. This is one of the areas management should investigate closely because reducing cancellations and returns can protect revenue without necessarily requiring additional customer acquisition.




## Recommendation
- The business should focus on reducing the high purchase drop-off rate by improving the customer journey from cart to checkout and testing incentives such as free shipping. 
- Since Instagram generates the highest revenue, it should remain an important marketing channel, while Facebook should also be monitored because it has the highest average order value. 
- The business should also investigate the reasons behind cancelled and returned orders, as they represent significant lost value. 
- High-spending customers should be encouraged to make repeat purchases through personalised offers and relevant product recommendations


## Limitation
The analysis is based only on the transaction data available in the dataset. It shows what happened, but it does not provide enough information to explain why customers abandoned carts, cancelled orders, or returned products. External factors such as competitors, market conditions, customer feedback, and marketing costs were also not included. Therefore, the findings are useful for identifying areas that need attention, but the possible causes should be investigated further before making major business decisions.




## Conclusion
The business is generating strong revenue, but there are clear opportunities to retain more of the value already being created. Improving purchase conversion, reducing cancellations and returns, and making better use of successful marketing channels and promotions can help increase revenue without relying only on acquiring more customers.
